# NextInML — Project Brief
*Draft v1.0*

## The Problem Statement

Every year, thousands of students in the Middle East, Africa, and the broader Global South finish their first machine learning course and hit a wall.

They've watched the lectures. They've done the assignments. They know what gradient descent is. And then they open a new tab and have no idea what comes next.

This isn't a knowledge problem. It's an **exposure gap**, a structural inequality in who gets to know what opportunities exist. A student at MIT doesn't need a website like this. Their professor mentions MATS in office hours. Their classmate went to AMMI last year. Their LinkedIn feed surfaces these things automatically.

The student in Amman, Khartoum, or Casablanca has the same talent and the same internet connection, but none of that ambient network. They don't even know to search. The opportunity exists. The information exists. The access does not.

**NextInML exists to close that gap.**

## What NextInML Is

NextInML is a **discovery and access platform** for early-stage ML students, specifically those who have completed foundational coursework and are ready for their next real step, but don't know what that step looks like.

It is not a course platform. It is not a job board. It is the layer that lives between "I finished fast.ai" or "Andrew NG courese" and "I know what I'm doing with my career."

The platform aggregates and contextualizes three core opportunity types:

- **ML Summer & Winter Schools**: immersive, time-boxed programs focused on practical, hands-on learning. Examples: M2L, MENA.ML, Data Science Africa, AMMI. The focus is specifically on the *practical learning tracks inside these programs*, not conference schedules.
- **Research Fellowships & Mentorship Programs** : structured programs that connect students with real research problems and mentors. Examples: MATS, SPAR, Pivotal Research Fellowship, MARS, CBAI Fellowship.
- **Internship Programs**: undergraduate-accessible roles at research institutes and industry labs. Examples: MPIA Student Summer Internship, similar programs globally.

What unifies all three is the moment they serve: **the transition from passive learner to active participant.**

## The Human Layer

A pure directory is a commodity. What makes NextInML defensible is the **editorial and community layer** built on top of the listings.

Every program on NextInML will carry honest, first-person accounts from students who actually attended, not marketing copy from the program organizers. What was the vibe? Was it actually beginner-friendly despite what the website says? Was it worth the visa stress? Is it for someone like me?

This human layer is the core differentiator. It cannot be auto-generated, it cannot be cloned, and it is precisely what the target student needs most. They don't just need to know AMMI exists. They need to know whether to apply.

## Target Audience

**Primary:** Undergraduate students globally, with a strong initial focus on MENA and Africa, who have foundational ML knowledge and are seeking their first structured research, learning, or practical experience.

**Secondary:** Graduate students in the early stages of their career who are navigating the research fellowship landscape for the first time.

The platform is **global in scope** — opportunities from and for students worldwide, in-person and remote — but **regional in soul**. It was built from inside the gap it closes.

## Scope & Boundaries

**In scope:**
- ML summer schools and winter schools (in-person and remote)
- Research fellowships and mentorship matching programs
- Undergraduate internship programs at research institutions
- First-person reviews and experience writeups
- Application guidance and deadline tracking

**Out of scope (for now):**
- General ML conferences (the conference itself, not the school inside it)
- Paid courses or MOOCs
- Job boards for senior roles
- Academic paper aggregation

## The Stale Data Problem

The single biggest failure mode for opportunity aggregators is stale data. Programs announce, deadlines pass, pages go unmaintained, and the site becomes a graveyard.

This is a first-class design constraint, not an afterthought. The maintenance model must be built into the platform from day one. Initial approach: a small editorial curation process combined with community submissions, with clear visual indicators of when each listing was last verified.

## How It Grows

**Phase 1 — Foundation**
Launch with a curated, manually maintained directory of 30–50 high-quality programs. Focus on MENA-relevant and globally accessible opportunities. Establish the review/experience layer with even a handful of honest writeups. Get the data hygiene right.

**Phase 2 — Community**
Open submissions. Let alumni write about their experiences. Build a small community of students who are actively navigating these opportunities. The platform becomes a living resource, not a static list.

**Phase 3 — Expansion**
Broaden the opportunity taxonomy. Introduce filtering by region, format, deadline, field of focus. Potentially explore mentorship matching as a direct feature — connecting students with mentors in a structured way, not just listing programs that do this externally.

**Phase 4 — Identity**
NextInML becomes the canonical answer to "what do I do after my first ML course" for students in the Global South. The name, the brand, and the community reinforce each other.

## The Name

**NextInML** — direct, self-explanatory, and accurately names the moment it serves. It answers the exact question the target student is asking: *what's next for me in ML?*

Domain: `nextinml.com` (to be confirmed)

## What This Is Really About

NextInML is not just an opportunity aggregator. It is a substitute for the network that talented students in underserved regions were never born into.

The information asymmetry in who gets to pursue serious ML careers is not a talent gap. It is an access gap. NextInML is the infrastructure that shouldn't need to exist — but does, because the ambient networks that carry this information to some students do not reach others.

That is the mission. The website is just how it starts.
